Your Projects Grew. Can Your Back Office Keep Up?

The back-office structure that worked when the company was smaller may not be enough as the business moves into a more complex stage of growth.

This is especially common as contractors approach the $5M–$15M range, where the number of projects, PMs, financial transactions, and management decisions can increase faster than the finance function supporting them.

Signs the operating model may be under pressure

The Owner is still too involved

Financial questions, reporting issues, and project exceptions continue to escalate to the Owner or President.

WIP does not tie out consistently

Project inputs may be stale, changes may not be reflected correctly, or nobody clearly owns the reconciliation process.

Job-cost information is late

The project team knows something changed before finance can show management the financial impact.

Reporting requires too much manual work

Spreadsheets, reconciliations, and workarounds become increasingly difficult to maintain.

The next answer always seems to be “hire another person”

Headcount can help, but it does not automatically solve the system, process, and accountability issues behind the work.

Assess the Operating Model

If Your WIP Doesn't Tie Out, That Is a Real Operating Problem

A WIP report can be wrong or difficult to reconcile for more than one reason.

The calculation environment matters, but so do the project inputs feeding it and the people responsible for reviewing the information.

Reliable WIP requires three things working together:

1

Accurate Calculations

The financial system needs the correct structure and calculation logic.

2

Current Project Inputs

Cost-to-complete information, project changes, job costs, and other operating inputs need to be updated consistently.

3

Controller-Level Ownership

Someone needs to review the information, work with PMs, reconcile exceptions, explain changes, and close the loop.

Accurate WIP is not just a reporting feature.

It is an operating process.
